Student rights and visa compliance

Studying abroad is a big investment. Knowing your rights, and the rules you have to follow, protects it.

13 October 2026 · 5 minute read · SA Global Education

Your rights as an applicant

  • To clear, accurate information about courses, fees and entry requirements.
  • To know how your agent is paid, and whether it charges you anything.
  • To pay universities directly, and to receive official receipts.
  • To read a university's refund and withdrawal policy before you pay a deposit.
  • To have your personal data protected and used only for your application.
  • To complain, first to the agent or university and then, where eligible, to an independent ombudsman.

Your responsibilities

Visa systems put the responsibility for an application on the applicant. Under UK rules, an application must be refused if the applicant uses deception, and a refusal for deception can lead to a ban on applying again for ten years. A false document can lead to refusal even if the applicant did not know it was false. Innocent mistakes are treated differently, but they can still lead to refusal if the evidence does not meet the rules.

  • Give accurate information about your studies, finances and travel history.
  • Declare previous visa refusals.
  • Use only genuine documents, and check your bank statements meet the rules for your destination.
  • Write your own personal statement.
  • Read every form before it is submitted, because the declaration is yours.

Why universities are checking more closely

From 1 June 2026, UK universities that sponsor international students must meet tougher Home Office standards: a Student visa refusal rate below 5%, at least 95% of offered students enrolling, and at least 85% completing their course. Universities that miss these levels can lose their ability to sponsor students.

As a result, universities are scrutinising applications, interview answers and finances more closely, and they are reviewing the agents they work with. Honest, well-prepared applications have never mattered more.

Refunds

If your plans change, or a visa is refused, any refund of tuition fees or deposits comes from the university under its own refund policy. You will usually need to request it yourself, within a deadline and with evidence such as a refusal letter. Read each policy before you pay, and keep every receipt.

Where to get independent advice

  • UKCISA, the UK Council for International Student Affairs, offers free advice for international students.
  • GOV.UK publishes the official rules for UK Student visas.
  • Universities' international offices can confirm offers, fees and refund terms.
